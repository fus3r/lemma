# Using Lemma

A tour of the reading and study features, in more detail than the in-app Lemma Tour. [The libraries](LIBRARIES.md), [the assistants](ASSISTANTS.md) and [the tour](TUTORIAL.md) have their own pages.

## Reading and study

Open a PDF (⌘O), drop one on the window or on Lemma in the Dock, or choose Lemma in Finder's Open With menu; each book opens in its own tab. The activity bar on the left switches the sidebar between **Books**, **Outline**, **Results**, **Symbols**, **Index**, **Lab** (notebooks and derivations), **Search**, **Marks**, **Pages** and the **Catalog**. The notebook on the right has **Page**, **Notes**, **Glossary**, **Context**, and **Review**; the bottom panel (⌘J) has **Problems**, **References**, **Hierarchy**, **Questions**, **Notes** and the **Console**. Editors beside the page, another page, a notebook, the map, a derivation, open as tabs of a second editor group.

Click an underlined reference to peek at it without leaving your page; right-click it to Go to, Pin or Find mentions. The peek shows the page at a little less than your reading zoom, within 70% of the window; drag its right edge, its bottom edge or the corner between them to make it larger or smaller, as with a window (a peek that opens above the reference is resized by its top edge and top-right corner instead), and the page refits to the new width. Drag a peek by its title bar to move it anywhere: it then stays open while you read on, until you close it (in the Classic window, drag the popover away from its reference). ⇧-click another reference to open its peek beside the ones already open, so that a figure and the theorem that uses it stay in sight together; ⇧-clicking a reference whose peek is open brings it to the front. Clicking the label of an equation itself, such as “(2.3)” at the end of its line, behaves like ordinary text. Turn reference links off in the layout menu or in Settings. Lemma finds numbered equations from labels at the end of a line, captions and algorithm titles at the start of one, and sections from the outline; a scanned page without a text layer has no links.

Select text or enable **Capture** and draw around an equation. **New note** saves the source as a question, definition, notation, or theorem. Write your reasoning, record assumptions, and choose **Preview** for formatted Markdown/LaTeX. Saved explanations are formatted by default and remain editable.

**⌘-click** a selectable term, choose **Look up** for a selection, or press **⌘⇧L** to inspect your saved glossary near the PDF. The source crop, dimensions, scope, assumptions, and saved work appear without replacing your working note, notebook search, or reading position. If several entries reuse a name, compare their scopes. **Open note** changes the notebook; **Go to source** moves the PDF and can be followed by Back. Add **Lookup names**, one term or symbol per line, to a Definition, Notation, or Theorem, for example `X` and `design matrix`. Single symbols and TeX-style spellings such as `X_j`, `X^T`, or `\Gamma` retain case. Names use literal text matching; this does not parse equations or infer which definition applies. On a scan, press ⌘⇧L and type the term.

**Pin** keeps an earlier passage in Context. Inspect it without moving the reader, or compare it with your current note’s source. Bookmark a page with `⌘D`; highlight a selection with `⌘⇧H` or underline it with `⇧⌘U`. Named marks and note markers stay linked to the original page. The layout menu offers continuous/two-page reading and can hide the personal annotation layer. The zoom menu offers Fit page and Fit width.

In a note, choose **Add link** to connect another note as a Definition, Assumption, or Related passage, and record how it applies. Expand the linked title to inspect its source and saved work while keeping the original note and reading page. **Open note** changes the notebook; **Go to source** moves the PDF explicitly. The target shows which notes refer to it. Deleting a note removes its links; Undo restores them.

Saved notes open with their reasoning in **Preview**, with Markdown and mathematics rendered. Choose **Edit** to change the text; selecting another note returns to Preview. Existing margin notes also open in Preview; a new empty margin note starts ready for typing.

**Map…**, or **Study → Dependencies & References**, shows incoming and outgoing relationships around the selected note. Inspect a node to follow its links without moving the PDF; Open note and Go to source remain explicit. These are your recorded relationships, not a verified or automatically extracted proof graph.

For a scanned PDF, choose **OCR → Recognize current page**, or capture a region and choose **OCR**. Recognition runs on this Mac. Compare the image with the editable text, correct it, then copy it or create a new source note. The saved transcription is labelled OCR and remains searchable and exportable. This recognizes prose; equations, symbols, and column order require review. It does not reconstruct LaTeX or add a text layer to the PDF.

To search the whole scan, open **Search → Pages → Index this PDF**. Lemma's helper extracts PDF text and recognizes image-only pages locally, gently, and pauses while the Mac runs on battery or is hot. Each finished page is saved separately, so Stop and Resume retain completed work. Search this PDF or all indexed documents in Library. Right-click an OCR result and choose **Review OCR text** to correct the index beside its source image without creating a note. **Related meaning** uses the Mac's available sentence models to retrieve prose in the query's language; unavailable models fall back to text matches. The same option searches your saved notes and explanations in **All notes**. Text similarity does not establish mathematical equivalence, and search covers the indexed pages and saved notes only.

**Annotate** (`⌥⇧⌘D`, or **Study → PDF Annotation Toolbox**) opens the personal PDF tools: Select, Pen, Box, Ellipse, Line, Arrow and Eraser. Drag to draw a shape; hold Shift for a square or circle, or to snap a line or arrow to 45-degree directions. Color, line width, solid/dashed outlines and translucent fills are available below the tools. Click a shape’s outline to select it, drag the outline to move it, or drag either of its two handles to resize it. The same settings edit the selected shape. Delete or the trash button removes it; the eraser removes the stroke or shape you click. Escape cancels a shape being drawn or moved; Done returns to reading. Shapes are also listed in the page inspector. Native Undo/Redo restores creation, movement, resizing, style changes and deletion, including after switching books. The layout menu switches **Original / My Edition**, and **Export My Edition as PDF** saves a separate copy containing shapes, ink, highlights and text-note annotations. Marks follow zoom and reopen with the document; the original file stays intact. Drawing uses the existing page area.

Choose **Try ridge** or **Try Lasso** in a note to open a local interactive experiment. Write a prediction before changing regularization or predictor correlation. The charts show coefficients and fitted values for a small deterministic synthetic dataset; the objective, seed, and data conventions are visible. Lasso adds squared-loss contours and an L1 diamond, with coefficients becoming exactly zero as the penalty increases. Record your observation with the note, then reopen it or start a new prediction. Exact collinearity identifies coefficient nonuniqueness. This is an illustration, not a reproduction of the book or a held-out performance evaluation. Study export includes the exact sample/path CSV files and a Python plotting script requiring matplotlib; run it manually to recreate the saved illustration.

Add a note to review (note menu, right-click, or the **Recall** section of the note) and open Review (`⌘⇧R`). Attempt an explanation before revealing the source and saved work; the source region, or only its cloze masks, is hidden in the reader during the attempt. Grade your own recall to schedule the next one; notes marked Revisit by earlier versions are due immediately. **Focus** (`⌘⇧F`) hides the side panes for reading; with a wide window, notes appear as cards in the page margin.

Choose **Try ridge**, **Lasso** or **k-NN** in a note for a local interactive experiment. The k-NN simulation follows ESL §2.3.3: ten Gaussian means per class, 200 training points and 10,000 test points, with Lemma's own random draw chosen to resemble the book's error levels. **New random draw** samples again; the export includes the training data, error curves and a matplotlib script.

Select a passage and press **⌘K** for a quick question with context, or choose **Get help** on an existing note to select Explain, Hint, Next step, Check assumptions, Build intuition, Worked example, Counterexample, Recreate figure, or Turn into code. The figure request asks for data provenance and a reproducible script, and requires a toy illustration to be labelled when the original data is missing. Lemma never runs generated code by itself: **Open code in Lab** copies an answer's Python into notebook cells, which run only when you run them. The question sheet lists every context item, passage text, section, cited equations and figures, glossary entries (names, notation and scope only, never your reasoning), linked notes (opt-in) and pins, with a checkbox, up to eight images. Review the exact draft before sending it to the assistant you chose: ChatGPT inside Lemma, your installed Codex or Claude Code, or the OpenAI, Anthropic or Gemini API with your own key. [The assistants](ASSISTANTS.md) explains each one and what it receives.

Reading, notes, lookup names, links, ink, OCR transcriptions, experiments, marks, pins, layout, recall attempts and schedules, cloze masks, and the thought for next time are saved locally. **Export study notes** produces Markdown with links between notes, review schedules, structured JSON, crop images, ink SVGs, and experiment CSV/scripts. Existing 0.1–0.5 workspaces load automatically (schema 6). Keep the source file available so Lemma can reopen it. Search indexes and the reference analysis (`Indexes/<fingerprint>/references.json`) are separate local files, and are not included in study export or question packets. Library covers are cached in `Covers`.
